# Motion

- Pace video and animated walkthroughs slowly enough to read. Keep spacing generous.
- Scroll-linked effects follow the scroll at once, with no lag. Related colors change together.
- Keep motion restrained. Use it at key moments, not throughout. End videos on the strongest brand motion.
- Give each brand its own motion. Do not reuse another product's animations.
- Brand videos tell a story. Do not just copy UI from the page.
- Use blend modes and shaders only when they stay fast, and make them configurable.
- Animate chart width changes with a transition instead of an abrupt jump. (Abrupt resizing is jarring.)
- Let users pick the click target and animation for each step in a sequence. (Sequences were not interactive, so users could not set their trigger or animation.)
- Animate video text with fluid, engaging motion, not a static white box behind each line. (A plain box behind the text each frame felt flat and not engaging.)
- Play intro animations once and hold the final frame on screen. (Repeating the animation felt cheap, and the finished state should stay visible.)
- Build animated entries in small groups that grow gradually, not as a full-screen flood. (A flood of elements felt cheap. Gradual build-up feels like the real thing forming.)
- Vary the timing of animated steps so they feel organic instead of evenly orchestrated. (Real processes take uneven lengths of time, and even timing felt fake.)
- Use square boxes, not circles, as the shapes in motion graphics. (Boxes fit the document and grid theme. Circles felt off.)
- Show a real process in motion, step by step, instead of using a generic entrance. (Motion should show how the work happens, not decorate the screen.)
- Do not load heavy animation on mobile devices. (Heavy animation is slow and drains battery on phones.)
- Scale animated graphics with the viewport so they stay large and fit at any screen size. (A fixed-size animation looked small and broke on other screens.)
